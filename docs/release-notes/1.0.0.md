Daybreak is a statistics application that runs on your own computer. You bring a
file and a question, and it gives you the answer twice: once in everyday words,
and once in the language of a methods section. Both come from the same numbers,
and both say what the result cannot tell you. Your data is never uploaded, and
there is no account.

This is the first full release.

## What you can do with it

- **Open almost any data file.** CSV, TSV, Excel, LibreOffice, SPSS, Stata, SAS,
  R, JSON, Parquet, and Feather. Eleven example datasets are built in, each
  with a method already chosen and the columns filled in.
- **Find a method without knowing its name.** Help me choose lists situations in
  plain words, such as two separate groups on a numeric outcome, and names the
  methods that fit. All 56 methods can also be browsed by question or searched.
- **Run 56 methods.** Descriptions, group comparisons, equivalence tests,
  correlation and regression, models for yes or no, ordered, and count outcomes,
  repeated and clustered data, factor analysis and structural equation models,
  rater agreement, survival analysis, time series with forecasts,
  meta-analysis, and thirteen machine-learning methods scored on rows held back
  for testing.
- **Read a result at a glance.** Every result opens with a short verdict and the
  few numbers you would quote, followed by the everyday explanation, the
  statistical description, and a figure.
- **Rely on more than a p-value.** Where a default prior is well established,
  Daybreak reports a Bayes factor beside the interval and weighs the two. That
  lets a result say that the data point to no effect, which a test of
  difference alone cannot do.
- **See what was checked.** Each result lists the conditions it could examine in
  your columns, which ones held, and which need a second look.
- **Take the work with you.** Read and copy the R code behind any result.
  Download figures in eight formats, reports as text, Markdown, HTML, Word,
  PDF, or LaTeX, the tables as CSV, or one bundle with all of it. A session
  file reopens the data, the choices, and the result later.
- **Prepare missing data.** A workspace fills gaps by median or most common
  category, nearest neighbors, random forest, or chained equations, on a
  working copy that can be put back with one press.
- **Use it your way.** Dark and light themes, palettes for three kinds of color
  vision difference and a monochrome one, full keyboard operation, and figures
  that never rely on color alone.

## An optional local model

A small language model can reword a finished explanation. It runs on your
computer through Ollama, it never sees your rows, and Daybreak discards any
rewrite that adds, drops, or changes a number. Setup is three steps inside the
app. Daybreak can use an Ollama you already have or keep its own copy in a
folder it can remove again, and each model is listed with what it is good at
and where it falls short.

## Getting started

1. Install R 4.2 or later. R 4.4 or later is needed to open saved sessions and
   `.rds` or `.RData` files.
2. Download `daybreak.zip` from this page and unzip it.
3. On macOS, open `launch_daybreak.command`. Anywhere else, start R in the
   folder and run `source("run_daybreak.R")`.

The first start installs the R packages Daybreak needs into your personal
library. A short walkthrough opens on the first visit.

## Good to know

Daybreak does not fit survey-weighted models, pool results across imputed
datasets, fit Bayesian models, or estimate causal effects. The method directory
in `docs/METHODS.md` states what each method computes, and
`docs/METHOD_AUDIT.md` records how each one is checked.
